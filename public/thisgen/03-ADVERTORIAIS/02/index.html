Liposuction Can’t Reach It. Neither Can CoolSculpting. Here’s Why.

Not a criticism of either procedure — it’s anatomy. A look at the one fat compartment that sits on the other side of the abdominal wall, and what’s actually been studied against it.

1. The Body That Used to Respond to Everything

He played competitively until 28, trained seriously until 33, and never once had to think about his midsection. That’s not vanity — it’s just what the years looked like. Effort in, a result out, reliably.

2. The Moment the Math Stopped Working

By his early forties, the same training that used to produce a predictable result was producing a different one. Less change where he wanted it. More accumulation in the middle. Nothing about the effort had changed. The return had.

3. From Athlete to “I Guess This Is Just Age Now”

Somewhere in there, the explanation quietly downgraded from a problem to solve into a fact to accept. That shift happens to a lot of people, and it usually happens before anyone explains the actual mechanism.

4. A Few Different Approaches, Competent People, No Real Answer

He tried a genuine handful of things over a few years — a procedure, a device, a couple of different training protocols. The practitioners were honest and skilled at what they do. None of it addressed the part that turned out to matter: location.

5. A Long Comment Thread on Why Some Bodies “Just Hold Fat There”

The internet is full of theories about stubborn midsection fat — hormones, genetics, sleep, stress. Most of them are partially true and none of them, on their own, explain why targeted procedures don’t seem to touch it. One clinician’s explanation, buried in a long thread, finally did.

6. The Secret Exposed: Two Compartments, One Wall Between Them

Everything that didn’t work was operating in front of a wall of muscle. The fat that mattered was behind it.

Subcutaneous fat sits under the skin, in front of the abdominal wall. Visceral fat sits behind that wall, packed around the organs. Line the usual methods up against that distinction and the pattern becomes obvious: liposuction removes fat from under the skin — in front of the wall. Cryolipolysis cools fat through the skin — also in front of the wall. Core training strengthens the muscle that is the wall; it doesn’t reduce what’s stored behind it, and spot reduction isn’t a real phenomenon regardless.

Every one of those methods does exactly what it says it does. None of them is physically capable of operating on the other side of that muscle. That isn’t a marketing claim. It’s where the tissue is.

THE SHORT VERSION
You weren’t under-trying. You were reaching for something that was never within reach.
Visceral fat has been studied as its own target — not with a scale, but with CT imaging, because the compartment can shrink measurably while total body weight barely moves. The compound with trial data against it doesn’t replace a hormone; it asks the pituitary to release more of the one you already produce, in its natural pulsatile pattern.

Not which procedure removes more fat, but which approach can even reach the compartment that’s changing my shape. Once that question is on the table, a physician-supervised evaluation is the next logical step — not another round of the same tools aimed at the same wall.

9. The Science Behind the Evaluation

Three things sit underneath everything else in this article. None of them are marketing claims — they are the actual basis of the trial data.

The Signal
Tesamorelin is a synthetic analog of GHRH (growth hormone–releasing hormone), a 44–amino-acid compound that binds the GHRH receptor on the pituitary gland.
It asks the pituitary to release growth hormone in its own natural, pulsatile pattern — it does not supply growth hormone from outside the body.
The Compartment
Visceral adipose tissue sits behind the abdominal wall, around the organs — a distinct compartment from the subcutaneous fat under the skin.
In the trials, it was measured by CT imaging, not by scale, because this compartment can change while total body weight moves very little.
The Lean Mass Finding
A meta-analysis of five randomized controlled trials found a significant reduction in visceral adipose tissue and trunk fat, alongside a significant increase in lean body mass.
That direction — lean mass increasing — is notable because rapid weight loss (including GLP-1–driven loss) is typically associated with some lean mass decline.

10. How the Studied Mechanism Works, Step by Step

Stage 1 — Restore the Signal
Tesamorelin binds the GHRH receptor on the pituitary and prompts a pulse of the body’s own growth hormone — the same signalling pathway that naturally declines with age.
Stage 2 — Target the Deep Compartment
The downstream effect studied in the trials is a reduction in visceral adipose tissue specifically — the compartment behind the abdominal wall, measured by CT scan.
Stage 3 — Protect What’s Already Built
Across the same trials, lean body mass increased rather than declined — a meaningfully different pattern from the lean mass loss typically seen with rapid, non-selective weight loss.

14. Who This Is NOT For

Read this before you continue

This evaluation is not appropriate for everyone, and a substantial number of people who request it are told no. It is generally not a fit if any of the following apply to you:

  • You are pregnant, breastfeeding, or trying to conceive
  • You are under 21
  • You are looking for a guaranteed cosmetic outcome, a specific number of pounds or inches, or a fixed timeline
  • You have an active malignancy or a history that a provider determines makes GHRH signalling inadvisable
  • You are seeking this as a substitute for treatment of a diagnosed medical condition, rather than alongside it
  • You are not willing to be evaluated by a licensed provider before starting anything

If any of these apply, a provider will tell you directly rather than proceeding. That is not a sales objection to overcome — it is the evaluation working as intended.

15. The Real Cost of Chasing the Wrong Variable

None of this is a criticism of diet, training, or any medication someone is already using. It's a question of what each approach is actually built to change.

What Each Approach Is Built to Change

Diet and general exercise: total body weight and overall composition, gradually, without compartment specificity.

Liposuction / CoolSculpting: subcutaneous fat only — fat in front of the abdominal wall. Cannot reach visceral fat.

A GLP-1 medication: substantial total weight loss, which is not compartment-selective and can include a meaningful share of lean mass.

A GHRH-analog evaluation: the one approach with trial data specifically measuring the deep visceral compartment by imaging — available only through a licensed provider, and only for patients who qualify.

16. The Real Reason People Give Up Before Getting Answers

Most people who look into this don’t stop because the science is unconvincing. They stop because the first thing they find is a page overselling a guaranteed result, or a supplement with no clinical data behind it, and it’s reasonable to walk away from that.

The actual limiting factor is narrower and less exciting: this is a clinical decision, gated by an evaluation, for a compound with narrow FDA approval and off-label use elsewhere. That’s not a marketing hook. It’s the honest shape of the thing.
