Looking at the color-coded cross-sectional imaging was an eye-opening moment:

A substantial portion of the weight I had shed wasn't stubborn midsection fat—it was lean skeletal muscle tissue from my shoulders, back, and thighs. Meanwhile, the deep, dense visceral fat packed behind my abdominal wall was still heavily concentrated around my liver and core organs.

My physician explained what had happened:

Generic calorie suppression triggers the body to break down available muscle protein for energy, while deep visceral fat requires specific nocturnal endocrine signaling to be mobilized.

When natural pituitary hormone output drops with age (declining steadily after age 30), the body loses the primary biological trigger that unlocks deep visceral fat stores during sleep.

Endocrinology researchers identified that restoring this natural signaling pathway requires targeted Growth Hormone-Releasing Hormone (GHRH) activation.

Clinical studies on the targeted peptide **Tesamorelin** showed significant, measurable reductions in deep visceral adipose tissue (averaging up to 18% on CT scans) while actively protecting the skeletal muscle mass essential for metabolic rate and long-term vitality.
