**[HEADLINE HOOK]**
For men over 40, one of the most frustrating aspects of aggressive weight reduction is the shift in body architecture.

**[SUBHEAD — O DILEMA DA PERDA MAGRA]**
After weeks of rigorous dieting or appetite-reducing protocols, limbs often appear leaner and narrower, shirts feel loose around the shoulders, but the central midsection remains a firm, unyielding dome.

**[A FISIOLOGIA DA SARCOPENIA]**
Endocrinologists refer to this as a shift in body composition balance:

When the body enters a sustained caloric deficit without proper nocturnal endocrine support, it readily catabolizes skeletal muscle tissue for fuel. This leads to reduced muscle density and a slower resting metabolic rate.

Deep visceral adipose tissue—the metabolically active fat located *behind* the abdominal musculature—does not respond primarily to calorie restriction. It is regulated by the nocturnal release of Growth Hormone-Releasing Hormone (GHRH) during deep Stage 4 sleep.

**[A CURVA DA SOMATOPAUSA]**
As natural GHRH output diminishes with age:
- Lean skeletal muscle becomes harder to maintain during dieting.
- Visceral fat remains hormonally locked in the abdominal cavity.
- Overall body proportion shifts toward higher relative visceral density.

In multi-center clinical trials, patients experienced substantial reductions in visceral fat volume without the muscle-wasting effects seen in generic crash diets.
