**[HEADLINE HOOK]**
Not all abdominal fat is biologically or anatomically identical.

**[SUBHEAD — A DISTINÇÃO ANATÔMICA]**
Most conventional wellness programs treat the midsection as a single tissue layer, but medical anatomy reveals two distinct compartments separated by the abdominal muscle wall:

• **Compartment 1: Subcutaneous Fat (Exterior):**
This is the soft, pinchable layer that sits in front of the abdominal muscles. It readily expands and contracts with changes in daily calorie balance.

• **Compartment 2: Visceral Adipose Tissue (Interior):**
This is the dense, firm fat stored *behind* the abdominal wall, surrounding the liver, intestines, and cardiovascular vessels. It pushes the abdominal wall outward from within, creating a firm, dome-shaped appearance.

**[O GARGALO DA PRIVAÇÃO CALÓRICA]**
When individuals undergo rapid calorie-reduction regimens, the body easily burns through superficial subcutaneous fat and active muscle tissue, but often leaves the deeper visceral compartment untouched.

Why? Because visceral fat mobilization is regulated by a specific endocrine switch: the nocturnal release of Growth Hormone-Releasing Hormone (GHRH).

Starting in our 30s, natural GHRH secretion declines by roughly 14% each decade. By age 50, the nocturnal hormonal pulse required to trigger visceral lipolysis has dropped significantly.

**[A ATUAÇÃO DO TESAMORELIN]**
**Tesamorelin** is a targeted 44-amino-acid peptide analog engineered to bind directly to pituitary receptors, restoring the natural nighttime signaling that targets deep visceral tissue.

In clinical trials, cross-sectional CT imaging confirmed an average 18% decrease in deep visceral adipose volume alongside the preservation of healthy lean tissue.
